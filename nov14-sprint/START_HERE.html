The answer

Everything runs, and nothing here proves an edge yet. The trading bot works end to end on synthetic bars and finished ahead of buy-and-hold in its one sample, but eight times the costs wipes that out. No NFL rule beat the vig on games it hadn't seen. The useful part tonight is practice: judge the bot's calls blind, and check whether a price is worth it at your own confidence.

Every number on this page comes from a check the coordinator ran itself on a scratch copy, 14 November 2026, 21:24–22:30 UTC. Paper only.

Trading bot · paper replay · synthetic bars

Two reviewers judged one call blind, and disagreed

One buy signal was frozen into a packet with only the bars up to that moment. Two fresh reviewers checked it without seeing what happened next.

Claude · challenge

The rule and arithmetic check out, but the packet's fill time contradicts its own fill rule. Read one way, the fill uses a price from 7 seconds before the decision existed. Read the other way, the packet misstates a one-bar delay.

Astra · agree

The buy follows the written rule; both moving averages match to six decimals. It could not verify the three-bar cooldown.

Both answers, stored as recorded with their time and model: review/PKT-7069f9b05f2a/. Codex's resolution: Independent_Resolution.md. Reviews are advisory and cannot place anything.

Practise on calls nobody has judged

The desk's Practice tab shows the bot's eight other buy signals one at a time, with only what the bot knew at that moment. You choose follow or pass and write one line on why. Only then does it show the chart and what the trade did. Each call is saved once and never changed. The Trading bot tab does the same for the reviewed packet, with both reviews side by side.

Open odds/price_helper.html in any browser. Type a price and how often you honestly think the pick wins. It shows the win rate the price needs, the paper value per $100 if you're right, and roughly how many bets it would take to prove it.

The ledger is for your own reads, and it refuses bad input

The paper ledger logs your own picks and passes alongside the rule picks. Each entry has a price and a reason, so passes get scored too. The coordinator ran it on a scratch copy, and it refused all of these: a duplicate pick, a quote over 12 hours old, a future-dated quote, a stake over 1 unit, a game that had already kicked off, a missing reason, a malformed time, and a closing price for 60.5 attached to a 54.5 ticket. A player relisted at 60.5 is logged as a separate pick from 54.5. Running the rules twice adds nothing the second time.

Know the limits
  • Lines come from a free nflverse archive. The time a sportsbook posted each line is unknown, so a line is a paper observation, not a price you could have taken.
  • Team markets only. You type in player props yourself, and their prices aren't checked.
  • A price outside your −170 to +150 range is recorded and flagged, not refused.
  • Kalshi-style event contracts are treated as unavailable in Louisiana. See nfl/LEGAL_CHANNELS.md.
Research · community leads

What other builders learned the hard way

92 Reddit leads from 25 communities, plus 21 primary sources checked directly. Reddit blocks this Mac, so the leads came through Gemini's public search. Codex opened three of them at source and they matched. Ten links (T4, T5, T10, T13, T16, T26 to T29, T34) were written by Gemini without a lookup and may open the wrong thread; reddit/LEADS.md names them at the top.

  1. Signal only on a closed bar. TradingView's own docs say that waiting for the bar to confirm avoids repainting. The Pine indicator and the paper runner both do this.
  2. Count every variant you try. The more rules you test, the more likely a lucky winner looks real. The NFL lane listed its ten up front and scored the held-out years once.
  3. Fill at the next bar, with costs. Never fill at the signal bar's close. Keep signal time and fill time separate, and show the result again at double the cost.
  4. Code does the numbers; the model only explains. A model can remember how past markets moved, so the model is never asked to judge past dates.
  5. One decision per opportunity. A rogue-agent post-mortem came down to two copies of the same task trading the same thing. Each decision is keyed and locked.

All leads, with the status of each and the pages inspected: reddit/LEADS.md. Codex's check at source: codex/Reddit_Source_Check.md. Codex's review of ten trading videos: Video_Review/video-review.html, and its field guide Paper_Desk_Field_Guide.pdf (both added at this folder's top level in Codex's release copy). Performance figures in posts are the authors' own claims.

How to use it · no typing

Double-click Paper Desk

Everything above runs from one page on this Mac. It needs nothing installed beyond the Python that comes with macOS.

  1. Open the desk. In this folder, double-click Paper Desk.command. A Terminal window opens and the desk opens in your browser. If you copied the folder from a download and macOS blocks it, right-click the file and choose Open.
  2. Judge the bot. The Trading bot tab shows the reviewed call with both reviews; the Practice tab gives you its other calls blind. Choose follow or pass, write one line on why, and press the button. The chart and the result appear only after your call is saved.
  3. Load Sunday's slate. Press Load the slate. Games that have already kicked off are left out.
  4. Log a take or a pass. Pick the game, the market and the side. Type the line for the side you picked (home −2.5), the price you saw, when and where you saw it, and one line on why. Press Log decision. If the ledger refuses an entry, the page shows the reason, for example a quote more than 12 hours old.
  5. After the games. Press Settle finished games, then Show my ledger for the record, including how your passes would have done.
  6. Stop it. Close the Terminal window. Your ledger stays in nfl/ledger/, and opening the desk again picks up where you left off.

What it does

  • Runs the same ledger tool the coordinator tested, with the same refusals.
  • Records your price as your own report, marked unverified.
  • Works from this folder wherever you move it.

What it never does

  • Place a bet or an order, or sign in anywhere.
  • Send TradingView data, or any data, off this Mac.
  • Answer anyone but this Mac (it listens on 127.0.0.1 only).

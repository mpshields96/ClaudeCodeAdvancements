What the book itself thinks

Type both sides of the same market. Their implied chances add to more than 100%; the extra is the book's cut. Scaling them back to 100% gives the book's own fair estimate, which your confidence has to beat.

Read this before trusting a number
  • Your confidence is the weak link. The arithmetic is exact; the input is a guess. In this sprint's NFL test, none of the four frozen rules cleared a 95% lower bound above the 52.38% that −110 needs on games it hadn't seen. The best one won 62.3% of 63 bets and still could not rule out break-even.
  • "Bets to prove it" is roughly how many bets you'd need, if you really win at your stated rate, before a 95% lower bound clears the price's break-even. It is a rough normal approximation, not a guarantee.
  • Paper only. Nothing here places a bet. Kalshi-style event contracts are treated as unavailable in Louisiana.

Formulas: a negative price −P needs P/(P+100); a positive price +P needs 100/(P+100). EV per $100 = confidence × win − (1 − confidence) × 100. Bets to prove it ≈ 1.96² × c(1−c) / (c − needs)².
